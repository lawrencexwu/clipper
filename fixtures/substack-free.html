The Future of Computing

Why the next decade will look nothing like the last

Bob Writer
May 1, 2026

The trajectory of computing over the next decade will surprise even careful observers. We are entering a phase of compounding capability that lacks historical analog, and the speed of change makes it hard to maintain a useful mental model for more than a few months at a time.

Three forces are converging at once. Model scaling continues to deliver real gains, even if the marginal returns are noisier than they were. Memory efficiency improvements are opening up workflows that were previously cost-prohibitive. And tool use — the messy, practical business of letting a model reach out and do things — is finally being treated as a first-class design surface rather than an afterthought.

Scale alone is not enough

Larger models still tend to do better at most benchmarks, but anyone watching closely can see the diminishing returns on raw parameter count in certain domains. The interesting wins now come from better training data, more thoughtful curricula, and smarter integrations with external tools. None of those scale with the same simple curve as parameters.

Consider what happened with code generation. The first wave of capable models produced impressive demos that broke under real workloads. Today's systems plan, run tests, and revise — a categorically different artifact. The difference is not really about size; it is about the system around the model.

Memory changes the equation

Long-context models combined with high-quality retrieval start to feel different to use in a way that is hard to describe until you try it. The handoff cost between sessions drops toward zero. Workflows that used to require a fresh setup every morning become continuous. The model remembers, more or less, where you left off and why.

This is not a small UX improvement. It is a precondition for trust. You cannot delegate meaningful work to a system that forgets the constraints you established five minutes ago. Persistence — even imperfect persistence — unlocks a different class of collaboration.

Where this leads

Predicting specific products is a fool's game, but the shape of the next few years is becoming clearer. We will see fewer monolithic chat interfaces and more agents embedded in the tools we already use. The distinction between "AI app" and "regular app" will erode the way the distinction between "internet app" and "regular app" eroded a generation ago.

The companies that thrive will be the ones that take this transition seriously without overcommitting to any single architectural bet. There is still real uncertainty about which approaches will win, and the right move for most teams is to build for adaptability rather than for any specific provider's roadmap.
